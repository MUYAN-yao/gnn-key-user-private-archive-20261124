Dear Editor of Computing,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Selection Resolution Changes
Candidate-Truncation Conclusions in Simulated Influence Maximization,”** as an
Regular Paper in Computing.

The manuscript concerns simulation-based network optimization and the
interpretation of computational comparisons, connecting to Computing's scope
in network science and its emphasis on systematic foundations of results.

The paper addresses a reproducibility problem in simulation-based network
optimization. Candidate truncation is commonly used to reduce the cost of
greedy influence maximization, but an apparent match with a full-greedy
reference can depend on the finite collection of Monte Carlo worlds used for
selection. We contribute a selection-resolution profile that jointly reports
nested selection-world re-optimization, round-level ties, same-budget
agreement with full greedy, independent evaluation and decision cost.

The empirical study uses a complete, frozen 42-condition matrix on four
openly licensed SocioPatterns temporal contact datasets. On the stated
simulated independent-cascade protocol, every full-greedy ordered seed sequence
changes between each adjacent selection-world budget (20→80 and 80→320). The
degree candidate pools reduce decision time but do not uniformly reproduce the
same-budget full reference. These results support a constrained methodological
claim about how candidate-truncation evaluations should be reported. The
manuscript explicitly does not claim a new approximation algorithm, universal
equivalence of a candidate pool, observed information diffusion, causal
influence, or superiority of degree screening or a graph neural network.

The manuscript is original, is not under review elsewhere, and will be
submitted only after all authors approve its content and the accompanying
declarations. The reproducibility package contains the frozen protocol, source
snapshot, input hashes, 42 condition-level outputs, validation report and
analysis code. Raw provider data will be accessed from the original
SocioPatterns sources under their stated terms rather than republished without
review.
