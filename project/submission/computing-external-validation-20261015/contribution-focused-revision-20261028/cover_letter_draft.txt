Dear Editors of Computing,

Please consider our manuscript, “Reference Budgets and Ranking Precision in Candidate-Restricted Influence Maximization,” for publication in Computing.

The manuscript addresses a difficulty in evaluating candidate restriction: a measured quality gap depends on a reference solution that is itself estimated, as well as on the numerical ranking that defines seed eligibility. We examine these dependencies through paired selection, separate budget and ranking-precision controls, and independent spread evaluation.

The central finding is a numerical dependence in an apparent budget effect: tightening PageRank tolerance exchanges two of 20 ca-GrQc candidates and removes the default mean gain-to-loss reversal. Two supporting findings quantify the spread consequence of reference changes and the limits of ranking transfer. In the historical high-budget comparison, 16 of 40 reference sets change while the mean spread gain across all 40 pairs is 0.01111 nodes. At the highest tested external budget, degree-based pools reproduce all 15 reference trajectories at lower selection cost, whereas PageRank losses persist on the directed graphs after tightening tolerance. These findings clarify when a quality comparison can be interpreted and what its computational savings preserve.

We consider this study relevant to Computing because it examines the interaction between numerical computation and the evaluation of network algorithms. The evidence comes from a small selected network sample. Three external topologies form the main comparison; four small contact graphs provide separate historical sensitivity controls. The manuscript retains unfavorable outcomes, identifies the precision analysis as post-hoc, and specifies the limits of the network, parameter and timing coverage. Reproducibility materials include source snapshots, configurations, saved outputs, analysis tables and validation records.

All authors have read and approved the manuscript and agreed to its submission to Computing. The authors declare no competing interests.
