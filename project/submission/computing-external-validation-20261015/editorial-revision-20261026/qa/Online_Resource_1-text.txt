1 Source-scoped related-work comparison
Table S1 compares the stated focus of accessible primary sources, rather than assert-

ing, candidate restriction and learned inductive estimation address different parts of
the influence-maximization pipeline. This study’s contribution is the joint evaluation
of reference budget, ranking precision, coverage, independent utility and cost—not a
claim that each component is individually novel. Complete citations are in the main
manuscript.

CollegeMsg retains the first⌊.65T⌋complete timestamp groups, whereT=58,911,

2.2 Finite-world coupling counterexample
Consider the chains→u→vwith horizon two. In one step-indexed world,s→uis
open at step zero, whileu→vis closed at step zero and open at step one. Starting
with{s}reaches all three vertices. Starting with{s,u}activatesuearlier and reaches
only two. Thus the finite-world objective is not necessarily monotone, even though
the marginal law for each fixed seed set remains the intended independent-cascade
law. This example alone does not establish a violation of submodularity. The fixed-
edge control instead uses horizon-limited reachability in each sampled graph, giving
a monotone, submodular coverage objective. Both implementations enumerate all
eligible vertices at every round. The external world-ID audit checks 169,200 distinct
identifiers across graph, path and selection/evaluation families for collisions.
3 Statistical interpretation
For a fixed selected pair, conditional Monte Carlo standard error is the sample stan-
dard deviation of 10,000 paired per-world differences divided by 100. Normal in-
tervals are pointwise and conditional. Across-path SD describes five computational
paths, or three control paths, and includes finite evaluation noise. The optionaltinter-
vals in CSVs are post-hoc descriptive intervals, not population-of-network inference.
Random pools are averaged within each path before cross-path analysis; their shared-
world covariance is retained.
Incremental spread subtracts five seeds from total spread. Percentage loss is minus
the paired mean difference divided by full incremental spread, times 100. The plug-in
percentage does not have a guaranteed loss-bound interpretation. The primary sign-
test family is three methods within each graph atW=1280. All nonzero PageRank

4 Cost and repeated measurements
The archived preparation timer contains parsing, all rankings, hashes and graph di-
agnostics. Its ranking subtimers are not added to it again. A separate post-hoc setup
study measures five repetitions of raw parsing/CSR and one method’s ranking plus
pool generation. The frozen selection timer already includes lexical candidate sort-
ing; it is not charged again. Hybrid deployment cost adds that median setup to frozen
selection-world preparation and selection. Hybrid verification cost adds independent-
world preparation and independent evaluation. These are assembled warm-process,
cached-file estimates, not direct end-to-end trials. Imports, JIT compilation, down-
loads and output writing are excluded.
Frozen repeated timing uses path 1101 andW=320, five randomized repetitions
per arm, yielding 130 observations. Selected sequences and per-world evaluations
were required to remain identical. The CSVs report every repetition and its selec-
tion/evaluation time, plus medians and ranges. The maximum positive psutil process-
lifetime peak is 3.448822 GiB; it must not be confused with an unverified PowerShell
display or with preflight measurements using one tenth the evaluation world count.
Timings are machine- and implementation-specific.

6 Post-hoc PageRank precision control
The frozen default tolerance is 10 −6, and NetworkX stops on a successive vectorL 1
change belowntimes that value. A candidate-only diagnostic at 10 −12 changed some
pool members on ca-GrQc and wiki-V ote; pools agreed between 10 −12 and 10 −14.
Before inspecting new utility outputs, a separate protocol froze two PageRank arms
on every graph and all five originalW=1280 paths. Its 15 conditions and 30 arms
are a numerical sensitivity control, not independent confirmation and not additional
paths in the primary tests.
Table S6 shows that tightening tolerance changes ca-GrQc’s 4k mean difference
from−0.25082 to+0.02608, replacing all five selected sets. Its original small loss
is therefore not precision-robust. CollegeMsg outcomes are unchanged. wiki-V ote’s
4k loss remains large (−41.21970, or 73.96% of incremental spread), and its 5% loss
is 24.47%. This diagnostic qualifies the ranking interpretation without replacing any
original observation or retroactively redefining the frozen sign-test family. Tightened-
solver timing is not substituted into the default-solver cost curves.

7 Post-hoc precision–budget control
The initial precision control left a material question open because it covered only
W=1280. A new protocol was frozen before new utility outputs were inspected and
added strict PageRank-4k and PageRank-5% arms atW=80,320 for all three graphs
and five original paths. It contributes 30 conditions and 60 restricted arms, reusing the
frozen full references and disjoint evaluation-world IDs. Together with the prior strict
W=1280 layer, this is a 45-condition, 90-arm post-hoc sensitivity study; it adds no
independent paths or primary tests.
Strict 10−12 pools agree with 10−14 pools for every graph/pool size. CollegeMsg’s
strict and default pools are identical. ca-GrQc changes two of 20 4k candidates (pool
Jaccard .818), while wiki-V ote changes one (Jaccard .905). The lower-budget results
show that ca-GrQc’s default PageRank-4k direction reversal does not survive the sta-
ble strict pool: strict losses are -11.25%, -2.96% and -0.54% atW=80,320,1280.

9 Descriptive utility–cost thresholds
Figure S3 retains all frozen loss thresholds. Degree-4k meets zero point-estimate
loss on every highest-budget path. Default PageRank-4k meets 10% on ca-GrQc and
CollegeMsg, but not 20% on wiki-V ote. The 5% PageRank pool meets zero loss
on ca-GrQc and 5% on CollegeMsg, and still fails 20% on wiki-V ote. No within-
path random-pool mean meets 20%. These thresholds are descriptive; no application-
specific acceptable loss or noninferiority margin is inferred. Strict-tolerance timing is
not substituted into this analysis.
10 Coupling control
Figure S4 shows all preselected W320 control paths for the main ranking compar-
isons. Mean PageRank-4k differences under edge coupling are−0.16240,−0.93417
and−40.57597 on ca-GrQc, CollegeMsg and wiki-V ote. Degree-4k ordered agree-
ment is 1/3, 3/3 and 3/3, respectively. This control preserves the prominent PageRank
losses while showing that coupling can change reference reproduction. It does not es-
timate a general coupling-invariance law.
