Reference Budgets and Ranking Precision in Candidate-Restricted Influence Maximization

The contribution is empirical: a paired numerical control changes the interpretation of a budget comparison on ca-GrQc, and independent evaluation identifies the spread consequences of reference-set changes. Cross-topology and coverage comparisons delimit where the observed candidate-restriction results transfer. These findings motivate reporting reference budget, ranking precision, coverage, seed agreement, independently evaluated spread and timing scope together.

The study holds the simulator and greedy selection rule fixed to examine eligibility and evaluation settings. Its distinguishing evidence is the precision-sensitive budget observation and its graph-specific qualification, rather than the number of benchmark conditions or a claim that each evaluation practice is novel. The manuscript does not infer a general theorem from these experiments. Quality and cost conclusions concern the stated finite-horizon setting and exhaustive reference implementation; the paired precision study remains post-hoc.

The historical-evidence revision integrates already completed budget, propagation-parameter, fixed-edge and coverage controls as supplementary support. It increases the visibility and traceability of existing evidence, not the number of experiments or independent networks. Historical small-graph parameter sensitivity does not establish sensitivity on the three external networks, and the fixed-edge control does not establish the strict-PageRank finding under a different coupling.
