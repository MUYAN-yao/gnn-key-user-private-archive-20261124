Reference Budgets and Ranking Precision in
Candidate-Restricted Influence Maximization
Jiayue Sun·Binbin Yong·Junjie An·
Tianyu Bo·Shuai Li
AbstractCandidate restriction reduces greedy search cost in influence maximiza-
tion, but evaluating its quality involves two numerical choices: the sampling budget
of the full-candidate reference and the precision of the ranking used to construct
the pool. We examine their consequences through paired selection and independent
spread evaluation on three real network topologies. On ca-GrQc, default PageRank
yields a mean incremental-spread gain of 5.61% at 80 selection worlds and a loss
of 5.07% at 1280. A post-hoc tolerance control removes this reversal, while budget-
dependent seed changes remain. The two directed networks retain PageRank losses
under stricter ranking. Changes in reference sets also need not imply comparable util-
ity changes: all five ca-GrQc sets change between 320 and 1280 worlds, with a mean
spread gain of 0.12720 nodes. At 1280 worlds, degree-based pools reproduce all 15
full-reference trajectories at lower measured selection cost. These results support re-
porting reference budget and ranking precision alongside candidate coverage, seed
agreement, independent utility and the scope of computational cost.

Two numerical choices enter this comparison at different stages. The full-
candidate reference is selected using finitely sampled diffusion worlds, so increasing
its budget can change both its seeds and the measured gap to a restricted method.
A ranking determines eligibility before selection: changes in numerical scores can
exchange vertices at the pool boundary and alter the restricted search. A quality
change observed across budgets therefore needs to be examined together with the
precision used to form the pool.
Sampling accuracy and scalability are established concerns in influence max-
imization [1–5]. Our question concerns the interpretation of candidate-restriction
results: which observed differences persist when the reference budget and ranking
precision are varied separately, and how do seed changes translate into independent
spread? We use a paired evaluation design (Fig. 1) that holds the graph, world prefix
and tie rule fixed across full and restricted selection, then evaluates the resulting seeds
on shared worlds independent of selection.
The external study uses communication, collaboration and voting topologies. Its
post-hoc PageRank control shows that ca-GrQc’s default gain-to-loss pattern across
budgets disappears under stricter ranking, whereas losses on the two directed graphs
persist. We then relate seed changes to independently evaluated utility and exam-
ine candidate coverage and measured cost. This organization separates a tolerance-
sensitive observation from results that persist in the tested control, while keeping the
original outcomes visible.
The study makes three empirical contributions:
(1)Distinguishing budget effects from ranking-precision effects.On ca-GrQc,
tightening PageRank tolerance removes the apparent budget-induced gain-to-loss
reversal while leaving budget-dependent seed changes. The paired control identi-
fies the precision dependence of this observation.
(2)Separating seed agreement from utility differences.Reference-budget transi-
tions produce set changes with differing spread consequences. Paired evaluation
quantifies their magnitudes and uncertainty, rather than treating a changed set as
sufficient evidence of a quality change.
(3)Qualifying transfer and computational savings.Degree-based pools reproduce
all 15 highest-budget external trajectories, whereas the earlier PageRank agree-
ment does not transfer uniformly. Coverage and scoped timing comparisons show
where the observed restriction preserves the reference and saves computation.

Ranking and search strategies offer complementary routes to efficient selection.
Neighbour-scale centrality [7] and discrete metaheuristic optimization [8], both stud-
ied inComputing, change how influential seeds are identified. Learned inductive es-
timators such as MONSTOR+ predict spread on unseen graphs [9]. Our comparisons
retain one simulator and greedy rule while changing the candidate pool, allowing
differences to be attributed to eligibility and evaluation resolution.
Benchmarking has established the importance of comparable models and imple-
mentation scope [5]. Temporal-network formulations [10] and empirical influence
models [11] also distinguish observed relations from diffusion assumptions. Our ev-
idence concerns simulated spread on observed topologies. The evaluation jointly
tracks reference budget, ranking precision, coverage, seed agreement, independent
spread and cost. Online Resource 1 provides a source-scoped comparison of these
dimensions.

3.1 Paired selection at controlled resolutions

activate each inactive outgoing neighbour with probabilityp. Propagation stops after
hrounds. For a random worldω, letf h(S;ω)denote total activated vertices, including
seeds. Greedy selection uses the sampled objective

3.2 World coupling and independent evaluation
Primary selection worlds share draws indexed by world, propagation step, source and
target. This coupling preserves the independent-cascade marginal law for each fixed
seed set, but the realized finite-world objective can be nonmonotone. For example,
initially activating an intermediate vertex can move its outgoing attempt to an earlier
step with a different sampled outcome. Online Resource 1 gives the explicit coun-
terexample. A separate fixed-edge control draws once per edge and world; its finite
objective is coverage by vertices reachable withinhedges and is monotone and sub-
modular. Both implementations use exhaustive enumeration.
Selection-world identifiers form nested prefixes asWincreases. Evaluation uses a
disjoint family ofM=10,000 worlds per path, shared by all seed sets and held fixed
across budgets. Keys also separate graphs, paths and candidate-pool randomization.
Coupling schemes are compared within their own full references.

3.3 Implementation and cost measurement
The Numba implementation stores candidate IDs, selected sequences, integer round
totals and per-world evaluation values. Audits verify source/data/result hashes, re-
construct argmax and tie decisions, replay full scores and evaluations, and compare
predetermined worlds with a separate scalar oracle. Identical seed sets must yield
identical evaluation vectors. The source snapshots, audit qualifications and repro-
ducible commands accompany Online Resource 1.1
Selection time includes greedy search and lexical candidate sorting, but excludes
graph preparation, world generation and evaluation. We additionally report a warm-
process verification-inclusive estimate: separately measured parsing, graph construc-
tion and one method’s ranking/pool generation are added to the frozen selection-
world, selection, evaluation-world and evaluation times. Setup uses the median of
five repeats. These assembled estimates exclude imports, just-in-time compilation,
downloads and output writing. Five randomized repeats at path 1101 andW=320
assess timing variability. Cost comparisons use this implementation’s exhaustive full-
candidate reference; strict-tolerance results are used only for quality sensitivity, not
the default-tolerance timing comparison.

4.2 Primary matrix and numerical sensitivity control
Table 2 specifies the external design. A condition fixes graph, path, selection bud-
get and coupling; an arm changes only seed eligibility. Three graphs, five paths and
three selection budgets define 45 primary conditions. Nine fixed-edge conditions pro-
vide a matched coupling control. Every condition includes full selection, degree-4k,
PageRank-4k, degree-5%, PageRank-5% and three random-4k pools; CollegeMsg
also includes historical-weighted 4k and 5% pools. This gives 18 conditions per
graph, with 10 arms for CollegeMsg and 8 for each static graph: 18(10+8+8) =468
arms. All 54 conditions were completed and retained.
The seed budget and propagation setting are held constant to isolate reference
budget, eligibility and coupling. They define a finite-horizon simulation regime rather
than parameters fitted to observed cascades. Resource-only preflight measured full
selection atW=80,320 and evaluation atM=1000; it recorded no selected sets or
utilities. Those measurements informed the feasible main matrix, whose evaluation
budget isM=10,000. The protocol was locally frozen before main outcomes were
inspected. No network or condition was excluded on the basis of its results. The
external budget ceiling is 1280; the historical 5120-world study is reported separately.

4.3 Randomization, execution and reproducibility
Randomization is specified by the configuration namespace, graph ID, path ID, world
family and zero-based world index. The frozen implementation hashes this tuple with
SHA-256 to obtain each 63-bit world identifier. Thus increasingWappends worlds
without resampling the prefix. The selection and evaluation families have distinct
keys; their identifiers are explicitly checked for overlap. A separate hash key con-
taining graph, path and repetition index generates each random candidate pool. The
pool remains fixed across budgets and coupling controls. Exact key strings and the
hash-to-draw mapping are preserved in the source snapshot and described in Online
Resource 1.
Experiments use one serial worker on Windows with Python 3.13.0, NumPy
2.3.3, NetworkX 3.5, SciPy 1.16.1 and Numba 0.68.0. BLAS/OpenMP threads are
constrained to one. JIT warm-up precedes timed conditions; randomized arm order
reduces systematic order effects. The recorded host has 20 physical CPU cores and
approximately 32 GiB RAM, although the implementation does not use all cores.
Timing and process peak-memory records accompany each condition. Exact wall
times are machine-dependent.
The reproduction entry point verifies frozen source and data hashes, rebuilds the
processed graphs and checks the result matrix before analysis. It supports a represen-
tative replay or the complete external matrix, writes to a new directory and compares
selected sequences, integer round scores and all evaluation counts with the archived
arrays. A separate stage regenerates default and precision-control statistics and plots
in an isolated copy. Numerical tables are checked against archived values, and raster
plots against decoded pixels; PDF timestamps and fresh execution times are not ex-

5 Results
5.1 The ca-GrQc budget reversal depends on ranking precision
Figure 2 compares default and strict PageRank at each selection budget, using
the same full reference and independent evaluation worlds. On ca-GrQc, default
PageRank-4k changes from a mean incremental-spread gain of 5.61% atW=80 to
losses of 2.53% and 5.07% at 320 and 1280. Tightening tolerance exchanges two of
20 candidates and yields gains of 11.25%, 2.96% and 0.54%. The mean gain-to-loss
reversal therefore disappears in this numerical control.
Budget still changes selections under the strict pool, whose membership agrees at
tolerances 10−12 and 10−14. Its selected sets change on 3/5 paths from 80 to 320 and
2/5 from 320 to 1280. AtW=1280, the mean paired difference is+0.02608 nodes,
with path values from−0.0408 to+0.1789 and path standard deviation 0.09155.
The mean is smaller than the path standard deviation; a positive sign alone does
not establish a utility advantage. Conditional Monte Carlo uncertainty is reported
separately in the per-path supplementary results.
CollegeMsg’s default and strict pools, seeds and evaluations are identical at every
budget, retaining losses of 9.33%, 8.52% and 9.01%. On wiki-V ote, one candidate
changes, but strict losses remain 74.05%, 74.05% and 73.96%. Precision sensitivity
is therefore network-specific: it changes the ca-GrQc interpretation while preserving
the two directed-network losses.
5.2 Reference-set changes have different utility consequences
Figure 3 compares reference-set changes with independent utility changes. FromW=
320 to 1280, all five ca-GrQc sets change, but their mean gain is 0.12720 nodes;
individual differences range from−0.0266 to+0.2411. CollegeMsg changes one set
and gains 0.05336 nodes averaged across all five paths. wiki-V ote changes no final
sets and has exactly zero paired gain, although some ordered sequences change.
Of the six changed sets, four have positive pointwise 95% conditional Monte
Carlo intervals; two ca-GrQc intervals include zero. The nine unchanged sets have
identical evaluation vectors. These comparisons separate the occurrence of a selection
change from its size and direction in utility. In the historical step-coupled comparison
fromW=1280 to 5120, 16/40 sets changed while mean gain was 0.01111 nodes
(range−0.0383 to+0.1180). Historical fixed-edge references also change: fromW=
320 to 1280, 29/40 sets changed, with mean gain 0.061845 nodes (range−0.0110 to
+0.2572). Each transition uses a shared evaluation family within its own coupling;
the two budget intervals are different and do not estimate a coupling effect. These
controls support separate reporting of set and utility changes, without establishing
convergence on the external networks.

6 Discussion
The ca-GrQc control changes the interpretation of the default budget comparison. A
two-candidate exchange under stricter PageRank removes its mean gain-to-loss pat-
tern, while changes across selection budgets remain. On CollegeMsg and wiki-V ote,
the stricter calculation retains the losses. These outcomes show why the budget com-
parison and the ranking-precision check answer different questions; neither control
substitutes for the other in this study.
The set-transition results add a second distinction. A different selected set estab-
lishes a change in the finite reference, but its utility consequence requires indepen-
dent evaluation. Similarly, degree-4k’s exact reference reproduction atW=1280 is
a statement about these computations, not an estimate of expected-optimality. Candi-
date coverage and propagation direction help interpret the contrasting PageRank out-
comes; the direction diagnostic remains an explanation consistent with the recorded
topology rather than an intervention-based causal result.
For evaluations using truncated rankings and sampled references, the evidence
motivates three reporting choices: give the actual candidate fraction, ranking toler-
ance and reference budget; report ordered agreement, final-set agreement and inde-
pendent utility with their appropriate uncertainty; and state which stages the cost
comparison includes. These quantities allow readers to distinguish preservation of a
reference computation from a measured utility difference and to interpret the accom-
panying savings.
The external evidence covers three selected networks underk=5,p=.01,h=3
and selection budgets up to 1280. Historical contact graphs additionally probep,h,
coupling andk, but their local parameter changes are not a full factorial design and
cannot establish sensitivity on the larger directed graphs. Coverage also limits inter-
pretation of the historicalk=20 control: the 4k pool contains all 75 Kenya vertices
and 80/91 invs2013 vertices. Kenya agreement is therefore a full-pool identity check.
The two synthetic controls are single fixed instances, not samples from their graph
families. Paths are computational replicates; the networks are not a probability sam-
ple of social systems. Observed messages, coauthorships and votes provide topology,
not calibrated causal diffusion. Reference agreement does not establish expected-
optimality or convergence, and the numerical control remains post-hoc. Cost savings
are relative to exhaustive selection in this implementation; assembled verification
costs exclude cold-start overhead.
7 Conclusion
The evaluation of a candidate restriction can change through either the sampled
full reference or the numerical ranking that defines eligibility. In the studied ca-

budgets without eliminating seed changes. Independent evaluation separates those
changes from their spread consequences. Across the three external graphs, degree-
based pools reproduce the testedW=1280 references at lower measured selection
cost, while PageRank’s results depend on precision, coverage and topology. Report-
ing these factors separately makes the basis and limits of the observed quality–cost
