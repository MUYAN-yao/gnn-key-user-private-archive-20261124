Title: Evaluating Candidate-Restricted Influence Maximization: Reference Budgets, Ranking Precision, and External Topologies

Scope and contribution
This manuscript is an evaluation study of candidate eligibility in greedy influence maximization. Its contribution is not a new influence-maximization algorithm, a GNN, or an expected-optimality guarantee. It separates two evaluation resolutions that can otherwise be conflated: the finite number of sampled worlds defining a full-candidate reference, and the numerical tolerance used to truncate a ranking before selection. The analysis carries those choices through candidate coverage, seed sequences, independently evaluated spread, coupling and scoped cost.

Evidence
The primary external matrix contains 54 frozen conditions and 468 executed arms on CollegeMsg, ca-GrQc and wiki-Vote (1,438--7,115 retained vertices), using k=5, p=.01, horizon 3, selection budgets W=80/320/1280, five paired computational paths and 10,000 disjoint evaluation worlds per path. At W=1280, degree-4k reproduces 15/15 stored full-greedy trajectories. Default PageRank-4k has mean incremental-spread losses of 5.07%, 9.01% and 73.01% on ca-GrQc, CollegeMsg and wiki-Vote. A separately frozen post-hoc sensitivity study contributes 45 conditions and 90 restricted arms across the original paths. It shows that ca-GrQc’s apparent default PageRank gain-to-loss change across budgets is not robust to stricter ranking tolerance; CollegeMsg is unchanged and wiki-Vote retains a large loss.

Interpretation and limits
The paper retains adverse outcomes and reports reference agreement, set changes and independently evaluated utility separately. The post-hoc controls are not represented as independent paths or primary confirmation. Five computational paths are not treated as five network samples; nonsignificant sign tests are not interpreted as equivalence. The study does not claim real-cascade validity, a universal safe candidate fraction, convergence at W=1280, or superiority over optimized influence-maximization systems.

Reproducibility
The submission package contains source and data manifests, expected hashes, frozen source snapshots, condition-level outputs, validators, analysis/plotting scripts and figure-source tables. Third-party raw source archives are obtained from their cited providers and are not redistributed.
