Reference Budgets and Ranking Precision in
Candidate-Restricted Influence Maximization
Jiayue Sun·Binbin Yong·Junjie An·
Tianyu Bo·Shuai Li
AbstractCandidate restriction reduces greedy search cost in influence maximiza-
tion, but evaluating its quality involves two numerical choices: the sampling budget
of the full-candidate reference and the precision of the ranking used to construct
the pool. We examine their consequences through paired selection and independent
spread evaluation on three real network topologies. On ca-GrQc, default PageRank
yields a mean incremental-spread gain of 5.61% at 80 selection worlds and a loss
of 5.07% at 1280. A post-hoc tolerance control removes this reversal, while budget-
dependent seed changes remain. The two directed networks retain PageRank losses
with a tighter PageRank convergence tolerance. Changes in reference sets also need
not imply comparable utility changes: all five ca-GrQc sets change between 320 and
1280 worlds, with a mean spread gain of 0.12720 nodes. At 1280 worlds, degree-
based pools reproduce all 15 full-reference trajectories at lower measured selection
cost. These results support reporting reference budget and ranking precision along-
side candidate coverage, seed agreement, independent utility and the scope of com-
putational cost.

Two numerical choices enter this comparison at different stages. The full-
candidate reference is selected using finitely sampled diffusion worlds, so increasing
its budget can change both its seeds and the measured gap to a restricted method.
A ranking determines eligibility before selection: changes in numerical scores can
exchange vertices at the pool boundary and alter the restricted search. A quality
change observed across budgets therefore needs to be examined together with the
precision used to form the pool.
We ask which candidate-restriction results persist when reference budget and
ranking precision are varied separately, and how seed changes affect independently
evaluated spread. Full and restricted selection share the graph, selection-world pre-
fix and tie rule (Fig. 1). Their selected seeds are evaluated on a shared set of worlds
independent of selection.
The external study uses communication, collaboration and voting topologies to
examine whether candidate-ranking results from smaller contact graphs transfer to
different network structures. A post-hoc PageRank control separates changes in pool
membership caused by solver tolerance from changes in seed selection caused by the
reference budget.
The study makes three empirical contributions:
(1)Distinguishing budget effects from ranking-precision effects.On ca-GrQc,
tightening PageRank tolerance removes the apparent budget-induced gain-to-loss
reversal while leaving budget-dependent seed changes.
(2)Separating seed agreement from utility differences.Reference-budget transi-
tions produce set changes with differing spread consequences. Paired evaluation
quantifies their magnitudes and uncertainty, rather than treating a changed set as
sufficient evidence of a quality change.
(3)Qualifying transfer and computational savings.Degree-based pools repro-
duce all 15 highest-budget external trajectories at lower measured selection cost,
whereas the earlier PageRank agreement does not transfer uniformly. Candidate
coverage and the included timing stages delimit these comparisons.

These lines of work address different questions: solver studies reduce the cost
of selecting seeds under a specified objective, ranking methods estimate which ver-
tices are influential, and benchmarking compares the quality and cost of implemented
methods. Our question concerns the interpretation of a restricted method’s gap to a
sampled full-candidate reference. Spread and runtime at a single configuration do
not identify whether that gap changes because the reference uses different selection
worlds or because ranking tolerance changes which vertices are eligible. We vary
these choices separately and retain paired seed and spread comparisons. This makes
the dependence of a quality judgment on the reference and the pool explicit, without
treating either as an exact expected-spread optimum.

3.2 World coupling and independent evaluation
Primary selection worlds share draws indexed by world, propagation step, source and
target. This coupling preserves the independent-cascade marginal law for each fixed
seed set, but the realized finite-world objective can be nonmonotone. For example,
initially activating an intermediate vertex can move its outgoing attempt to an earlier
step with a different sampled outcome. Online Resource 1 gives the explicit coun-
terexample. A separate fixed-edge control draws once per edge and world; its finite
objective is coverage by vertices reachable withinhedges and is monotone and sub-
modular. Both implementations use exhaustive enumeration.
Selection-world identifiers form nested prefixes asWincreases. Evaluation uses a
disjoint family ofM=10,000 worlds per path, shared by all seed sets and held fixed
across budgets. Keys also separate graphs, paths and candidate-pool randomization.
Coupling schemes are compared within their own full references.
For restricted seedsS C and full-reference seedsS F, define the paired evaluation
difference on worldiasd i =f h(SC;η i)−f h(SF;η i). The utility gap in Fig. 1 is

3.3 Implementation and cost measurement
The Numba implementation stores candidate IDs, selected sequences, integer round
totals and per-world evaluation values. The archived audits reconstruct argmax and tie
decisions, replay external full-reference scores and evaluations, and compare prede-
termined worlds with a separate scalar oracle. Identical seed sets must yield identical
evaluation vectors. Online Resource 1 details the audit coverage, the retrospective
correction to raw-file hash enforcement and the executable reproduction procedure.1
Selection time includes greedy search and lexical candidate sorting, but excludes
graph preparation, world generation and evaluation. We also estimate the cost of a
warm-process run that includes independent evaluation. Separately measured pars-
ing, graph construction and one method’s ranking/pool generation are added to the
recorded costs of selection-world preparation, seed selection, evaluation-world prepa-
ration and evaluation. Setup uses the median of five repeats. These assembled esti-
mates exclude imports, just-in-time compilation, downloads and output writing.
Five randomized repeats at path 1101 andW=320 assess timing variability. Cost
comparisons use this implementation’s exhaustive full-candidate reference. Results
at the tighter PageRank tolerance are used only for quality sensitivity; the timing
comparison uses default tolerance.

4.3 Randomization, execution and reproducibility
World identifiers separate graphs, paths and selection/evaluation families. Increasing
Wappends selection worlds without resampling the prefix; the selection and eval-
uation identifiers are checked for overlap. Random candidate pools use a separate
randomization key and remain fixed across budgets and coupling controls. Online
Resource 1, “Executable reproduction procedure”, specifies the exact hash keys, iden-
tifier construction and hash-to-draw mapping.
Experiments use one serial worker on Windows with Python 3.13.0, NumPy
2.3.3, NetworkX 3.5, SciPy 1.16.1 and Numba 0.68.0. BLAS/OpenMP threads are
constrained to one. JIT warm-up precedes timed conditions; randomized arm order
reduces systematic order effects. The recorded host has 20 physical CPU cores and
approximately 32 GiB RAM, although the implementation does not use all cores.
Timing and process peak-memory records accompany each condition. Exact wall
times are machine-dependent.

5 Results
5.1 The ca-GrQc budget reversal depends on ranking precision
Figure 2 compares PageRank at default and tighter tolerances at each selection bud-
get, using the same full reference and independent evaluation worlds. On ca-GrQc,
default PageRank-4k changes from a mean incremental-spread gain of 5.61% at
W=80 to losses of 2.53% and 5.07% at 320 and 1280. Tightening tolerance ex-
changes two of 20 candidates and yields gains of 11.25%, 2.96% and 0.54%. The
mean gain-to-loss reversal therefore disappears in this numerical control.
Budget still changes selections with the pool formed at the tighter tolerance,
whose membership agrees at tolerances 10−12 and 10−14. Its selected sets change on
3/5 paths from 80 to 320 and 2/5 from 320 to 1280. AtW=1280, PageRank-4k at the
tighter tolerance minus the same-budget full reference has a mean paired difference
of+0.02608 nodes, with path values from−0.0408 to+0.1789 and path standard
deviation 0.09155. The positive mean includes both gains and losses across paths; it
does not by itself establish a utility advantage. Conditional Monte Carlo uncertainty
is reported separately in the per-path supplementary results.
CollegeMsg’s default- and tighter-tolerance pools, seeds and evaluations are iden-
tical at every budget, retaining losses of 9.33%, 8.52% and 9.01%. On wiki-V ote, one
candidate changes, but losses at the tighter tolerance remain 74.05%, 74.05% and
73.96%. Precision sensitivity is therefore network-specific: it changes the ca-GrQc
interpretation while preserving the two directed-network losses.

6 Discussion
6.1 Interpreting budget and tolerance effects
The ca-GrQc control changes how the budget comparison should be read. The default
mean gain-to-loss pattern cannot be attributed to reference budget independently of
the PageRank stopping tolerance: exchanging two pool members removes that pat-
tern, although increasingWstill changes selected sets. Pool truncation turns a numer-
ical ranking change into a change in seed eligibility. Agreement of pool membership
at 10−12 and 10−14 narrows this diagnostic to the tested cutoffs; it does not certify an
exact ranking. The persistent CollegeMsg and wiki-V ote losses show why the toler-
ance check must be interpreted separately for each graph.
6.2 Interpreting seed agreement and spread differences
Ordered trajectories, final sets and spread answer different questions. A different se-
lection order can end at the same set, which produces the same evaluation vector
under the shared simulator and evaluation worlds. If sets differ, paired evaluation de-
termines the sign, magnitude and conditional uncertainty of the spread difference.
The external ca-GrQc mean change of 0.12720 nodes and the historical step-coupled
mean change of 0.01111 nodes quantify distinct budget transitions; neither supplies
an application-specific equivalence margin. Reporting the three outcomes separately
avoids treating every reference change as a utility improvement. Candidate fraction,
ranking tolerance and reference budget specify which comparison those outcomes
describe.

6.3 Scope and limitations
The external evidence covers three selected networks underk=5,p=.01,h=3 and
selection budgets up to 1280. Historical contact graphs also probep,h, coupling and
k, but their local parameter changes are not a full factorial design and do not establish
sensitivity on the larger directed graphs. At historicalk=20, the 4k pool contains
all 75 Kenya vertices and 80/91 invs2013 vertices; Kenya agreement is a full-pool
identity check. The historical fixed-edge controls also do not validate the external
tighter-tolerance PageRank results under fixed-edge sampling.
Paths repeat computations on a fixed graph, and the networks are not a probability
sample of social systems. Each synthetic control uses one fixed graph, so repeated
paths do not add graph instances. Conditional Monte Carlo uncertainty concerns
fixed selected sets; path variation concerns the computational repetitions, not cross-
network generalization. Observed relations provide topology, not calibrated causal
diffusion. The direction diagnostic is consistent with the measured wiki-V ote loss but
does not establish a causal explanation.
Reference agreement establishes neither optimality with respect to expected
spread nor convergence as the world budget increases. The cost comparison uses this
implementation’s exhaustive full-candidate reference, not CELF or IMM. Measured
selection costs and assembled costs including independent evaluation answer differ-
ent deployment questions. The latter exclude cold-start overhead and are not direct
end-to-end timings; tighter-tolerance PageRank is assessed for quality only.
7 Conclusion
On ca-GrQc, PageRank with a tighter convergence tolerance removes the default
mean quality reversal across budgets without eliminating seed changes. Across the
three external graphs, Degree-4k reproduces the testedW=1280 reference trajecto-
ries at lower measured selection cost, while PageRank’s results vary with tolerance,
coverage and topology. Under the shared simulator and evaluation worlds, identical
final sets yield identical spread evaluations; different sets require paired evaluation to
quantify the utility difference. Ordered-trajectory agreement, final-set agreement and
spread should therefore be reported separately. Agreement with the finite reference
does not establish optimality with respect to expected spread or performance beyond
the tested settings.
