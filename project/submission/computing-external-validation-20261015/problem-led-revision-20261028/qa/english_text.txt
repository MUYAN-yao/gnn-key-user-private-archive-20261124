Reference Budgets and Ranking Precision in
Candidate-Restricted Influence Maximization
Jiayue Sun·Binbin Yong·Junjie An·
Tianyu Bo·Shuai Li
AbstractCandidate restriction can reduce influence-maximization search costs, yet
its apparent quality depends on a reference solution that is itself estimated. A mea-
sured gain or loss may reflect reference sampling, numerical changes in candidate
eligibility, or both. We use paired selection and independent spread evaluation to dis-
tinguish these effects by varying reference budget and ranking precision separately.
Evidence comes from a small, selected sample of real-world networks: three external
topologies, with four small contact graphs providing separate controls. On ca-GrQc,
increasing the reference budget changes mean incremental spread from a 5.61% gain
to a 5.07% loss. This reversal disappears in a post-hoc control that tightens PageRank
tolerance. Budget-dependent seed changes persist, and reference-set changes have
different utility consequences. Across the external networks, degree-based pools re-
produce all 15 highest-budget reference trajectories, whereas PageRank losses persist
on the two directed networks. These findings identify how numerical choices can alter
judgments of candidate restriction. Reporting candidate-pool stability and indepen-
dently evaluated spread alongside reference agreement makes the basis of a measured
quality–cost trade-off explicit.

interpretation depends on how both were obtained.
Reference sampling and candidate ranking enter this comparison at different
stages. Increasing the sampling budget can change the full-reference seeds and the
measured gap to a restricted method. Ranking precision acts before greedy selection,
where small score changes can exchange vertices at the candidate-pool boundary. A
change in the apparent quality of a restricted method may therefore depend on either
numerical choice. Seed disagreement alone cannot resolve this ambiguity because
different sets can have similar independently evaluated spread.
We address this evaluation problem by separating the construction of the refer-
ence, the construction of the candidate pool and the assessment of selected seeds. The
analysis asks whether an apparent budget effect survives a ranking-precision control
and how reference-set changes translate into spread differences. Full and restricted
selection share the propagation graph, sampled-world prefix and tie rule (Fig. 1). In-
dependent evaluation then measures their paired utility difference. This design makes
each comparison explicit while keeping seed eligibility as the only difference be-
tween the two selection arms.
The evidence is drawn from a small, selected sample of real-world networks.
Three external communication, collaboration and voting topologies test the transfer
of earlier findings from four small contact graphs. The contact-graph results provide
separate budget, propagation-parameter and coupling controls. A post-hoc PageRank
analysis tests whether the external budget comparison depends on solver tolerance.
The main contributions are:
(1)An apparent budget reversal can depend on ranking precision.On ca-GrQc, a
tighter PageRank tolerance removes the mean gain-to-loss reversal across budgets
while seed changes persist. The comparison identifies a numerical dependence
that would be missed by interpreting the default budget scan alone.
(2)Reference-set changes need a separate utility assessment.The external and
historical budget comparisons produce set changes with differing spread con-
sequences. Paired independent evaluation quantifies those differences and their
conditional uncertainty, so seed disagreement is not used as a proxy for utility
loss.
(3)Reference-preserving savings have a defined scope.Degree-based pools repro-
duce all 15 highest-budget external trajectories at lower measured selection cost,
whereas earlier PageRank agreement does not transfer uniformly. Coverage and
direction diagnostics help interpret these differences; the savings apply to the
tested exhaustive-selection implementation.

3 Methods
3.1 Paired selection at controlled resolutions
LetG= (V,E)be a directed propagation graph andSa seed set of sizek. Under
the independent-cascade model, each newly activated vertex gets one opportunity to
activate each inactive outgoing neighbour with probabilityp. Propagation stops after
hrounds. For a random worldω, letf h(S;ω)denote total activated vertices, including
seeds. Greedy selection uses the sampled objective
bσW (S) = 1W W∑w=1 fh(S;ω w).(1)
At each round, greedy selection enumerates every unselected eligible vertex and
chooses the largest integer totalW bσW . Ties resolve by lexicographic original node
ID. Full selection usesC=V; restricted selection usesC⊆V. All vertices remain in
the propagation graph. A path identifies one computational repetition and its random-
world families. A condition fixes the graph, path and experimental settings; an arm
specifies a seed-eligibility rule within that condition. Full and restricted arms share
G, the world prefix,kand the tie rule.
We vary reference resolution throughWand ranking resolution through the nu-
merical toleranceτ. Degree ranks distinct outgoing neighbours. PageRank uses the
unweighted directed topology and NetworkX 3.5 defaults: damping .85, uniform per-
sonalization and dangling-node treatment, tolerance 10 −6 and at most 100 iterations
[12]. Its stopping criterion is successive-vectorL 1 change belownτ, wheren=|V|.
Scores are sorted decreasingly with lexical ties. A top-qpool containsq=min(n,4k)
orq=⌈.05n⌉vertices. Historical weighted degree counts outgoing observed events
where such counts exist. Three random pools are independently generated within
each graph/path and fixed across budgets and coupling controls.
3.2 World coupling and independent evaluation
Primary selection worlds share draws indexed by world, propagation step, source and
target. This coupling preserves the independent-cascade marginal law for each fixed
seed set, but the realized finite-world objective can be nonmonotone. For example,
initially activating an intermediate vertex can move its outgoing attempt to an earlier
step with a different sampled outcome. Online Resource 1 gives the explicit coun-
terexample. A separate fixed-edge control draws once per edge and world; its finite
objective is coverage by vertices reachable withinhedges and is monotone and sub-
modular. Both implementations use exhaustive enumeration.
Selection-world identifiers form nested prefixes asWincreases. Evaluation uses a
disjoint family ofM=10,000 worlds per path, shared by all seed sets and held fixed
across budgets. Keys also separate graphs, paths and candidate-pool randomization.
Coupling schemes are compared within their own full references.

3.3 Implementation and cost measurement
The Numba implementation stores candidate IDs, selected sequences, integer round
totals and per-world evaluation values. The archived audits reconstruct argmax and tie
decisions, replay external full-reference scores and evaluations, and compare prede-
termined worlds with a separate scalar oracle. Identical seed sets must yield identical
evaluation vectors. Online Resource 1 details the audit coverage, the retrospective
correction to raw-file hash enforcement and the executable reproduction procedure.1
Selection time includes greedy search and lexical candidate sorting, but excludes
graph preparation, world generation and evaluation. We also estimate the cost of a
warm-process run that includes independent evaluation. Separately measured pars-
ing, graph construction and one method’s ranking/pool generation are added to the
recorded costs of selection-world preparation, seed selection, evaluation-world prepa-
ration and evaluation. Setup uses the median of five repeats. These assembled esti-
mates exclude imports, just-in-time compilation, downloads and output writing.
Five randomized repeats at path 1101 andW=320 assess timing variability. Cost
comparisons use this implementation’s exhaustive full-candidate reference. Results
at the tighter PageRank tolerance are used only for quality sensitivity; the timing
comparison uses default tolerance.

5 Results
5.1 An apparent budget reversal depends on ranking precision
Figure 2 compares PageRank at default and tighter tolerances at each selection bud-
get, using the same full reference and independent evaluation worlds. On ca-GrQc,
default PageRank-4k changes from a mean incremental-spread gain of 5.61% at
W=80 to losses of 2.53% and 5.07% at 320 and 1280. Tightening tolerance ex-
changes two of 20 candidates and yields gains of 11.25%, 2.96% and 0.54%. The
mean gain-to-loss reversal therefore disappears in this numerical control.
Budget still changes selections with the pool formed at the tighter tolerance,
whose membership agrees at tolerances 10−12 and 10−14. Its selected sets change on
3/5 paths from 80 to 320 and 2/5 from 320 to 1280. AtW=1280, PageRank-4k at the
tighter tolerance minus the same-budget full reference has a mean paired difference
of+0.02608 nodes, with path values from−0.0408 to+0.1789 and path standard
deviation 0.09155. The positive mean includes both gains and losses across paths; it
does not by itself establish a utility advantage. Conditional Monte Carlo uncertainty
is reported separately in the per-path supplementary results.
CollegeMsg’s default- and tighter-tolerance pools, seeds and evaluations are iden-
tical at every budget, retaining losses of 9.33%, 8.52% and 9.01%. On wiki-V ote, one
candidate changes, but losses at the tighter tolerance remain 74.05%, 74.05% and
73.96%. Precision sensitivity is therefore network-specific: it changes the ca-GrQc
interpretation while preserving the two directed-network losses.

6 Discussion
6.1 What a budget comparison can establish
The ca-GrQc result changes the interpretation of an apparent budget effect. At default
PageRank tolerance, increasing the reference budget changes the restricted method’s
mean comparison from a gain to a loss. Tightening tolerance exchanges two pool
members and removes that pattern, although increasingWstill changes selected sets.
Thus, the default reversal is not evidence for a budget effect that is independent of
ranking precision. The candidate cutoff makes numerical ranking differences relevant
to seed eligibility. Pool agreement at 10 −12 and 10−14 checks membership stability
at the tested tolerances, without certifying an exact ranking. Losses persist on Col-
legeMsg and wiki-V ote, so this explanation does not account for every unfavorable
PageRank comparison.
6.2 From seed agreement to a quality–cost judgment
Reference agreement is useful for describing what candidate restriction preserves, but
it does not replace spread evaluation. A changed trajectory may end at the same set,
which gives the same evaluation vector under shared simulation worlds. When sets
differ, paired evaluation measures the sign, magnitude and conditional uncertainty

of their utility difference. The external ca-GrQc mean change of 0.12720 nodes and
the historical step-coupled mean change of 0.01111 nodes illustrate distinct budget
transitions. Neither value defines an application-specific equivalence margin. For cost
comparisons, agreement and independent spread should therefore accompany candi-

6.3 Scope and limitations
The external evidence covers three selected networks underk=5,p=.01,h=3 and
selection budgets up to 1280. Historical contact graphs also probep,h, coupling and
k, but their local parameter changes are not a full factorial design and do not establish
sensitivity on the larger directed graphs. At historicalk=20, the 4k pool contains
all 75 Kenya vertices and 80/91 invs2013 vertices; Kenya agreement is a full-pool
identity check. The historical fixed-edge controls also do not validate the external
tighter-tolerance PageRank results under fixed-edge sampling.
Paths repeat computations on a fixed graph, and the networks are not a probability
sample of social systems. Each synthetic control uses one fixed graph, so repeated
paths do not add graph instances. Conditional Monte Carlo uncertainty concerns
fixed selected sets; path variation concerns the computational repetitions, not cross-
network generalization. Observed relations provide topology, not calibrated causal
diffusion. The direction diagnostic is consistent with the measured wiki-V ote loss but
does not establish a causal explanation.
Reference agreement establishes neither optimality with respect to expected
spread nor convergence as the world budget increases. The cost comparison uses this
implementation’s exhaustive full-candidate reference, not CELF or IMM. Measured
selection costs and assembled costs including independent evaluation answer differ-
ent deployment questions. The latter exclude cold-start overhead and are not direct
end-to-end timings; tighter-tolerance PageRank is assessed for quality only.
7 Conclusion
Candidate-restriction quality must be interpreted relative to both a sampled reference
and a numerically constructed candidate pool. The ca-GrQc control shows why these
dependencies matter: tightening PageRank tolerance removes the apparent budget-
induced quality reversal, but budget-dependent seed changes remain. Independent
spread evaluation determines the utility consequences of those changes. Across the
external sample, Degree-4k preserves the testedW=1280 trajectories at lower mea-
sured selection cost, while PageRank outcomes depend on precision, coverage and
network context. The practical contribution is to make these dependencies testable
within a paired comparison, so reference agreement and utility retention are not con-
flated. The conclusions concern the studied networks and settings; matching a finite
reference does not establish expected-spread optimality.
