Evaluating Candidate-Restricted Influence Maximization:
Reference Budgets, Ranking Precision, and External
Topologies
Jiayue Sun·Binbin Yong·Junjie An·
Tianyu Bo·Shuai Li
AbstractRestricting seed candidates reduces the cost of influence maximization,
but its measured quality depends on both the sampled reference and the numerical
ranking used to construct the pool. We develop a paired evaluation framework that
separates these two resolutions and relates seed agreement to independent spread and
computational cost. Experiments on three real network topologies use nested selec-
tion budgets, shared random worlds and independent spread evaluation; a post-hoc
control isolates ranking precision. On ca-GrQc, default PageRank changes from a
5.61% gain to a 5.07% loss as the reference budget increases. Tightening the solver
tolerance removes this reversal, while budget-dependent seed changes remain. At
the highest tested budget, degree-based pools of 20 candidates reproduce all 15 full-
greedy trajectories and reduce measured selection time by 98.40–99.67% relative to
exhaustive full-candidate selection. PageRank losses on the directed networks per-
sist under tighter precision. These findings distinguish reference reproduction from
utility preservation and identify budget, ranking precision and candidate coverage as
necessary context for interpreting restriction quality.

budget, however, the reference is itself a sampled computation: increasing its budget
can change the selected seeds and the apparent loss of a restricted method. Reproduc-
ing the reference, preserving independently evaluated spread and saving computation
are therefore distinct outcomes.
A second source of variation arises before greedy selection. A numerical ranking
can change only slightly while exchanging vertices at the candidate-pool boundary.
Those exchanges can change the final seeds. Coverage and topology further compli-
cate the comparison: a pool containing most of a small contact graph may contain
less than one percent of a larger network, and incoming PageRank importance need
not align with outgoing simulated spread.
Sampling accuracy and scalability are established concerns in influence maxi-
mization [1–5]. We examine how reference budget and ranking precision jointly af-
fect the evaluation of a truncated candidate pool. Our paired design holds the prop-
agation graph, selection worlds and tie rule fixed across full and restricted greedy
selection, then evaluates both seed sets on shared, independent worlds (Fig. 1). This
design connects the source of a selection change to its utility and cost consequences.
The study provides three contributions:
–We identify a ranking-precision dependence in an apparent budget-induced qual-
ity reversal: tightening PageRank tolerance removes the ca-GrQc reversal, al-
though budget-dependent seed changes persist.
–We test the transfer of candidate-ranking results to three larger real topologies.
Degree-based pools retain full-reference agreement at the highest tested budget,
whereas PageRank’s earlier contact-graph agreement does not transfer uniformly.
–We quantify how reference-set changes translate into independent spread differ-
ences and how candidate restriction trades utility against measured selection cost.

2 Related work
Classical independent-cascade influence maximization connects expected spread
to greedy seed selection [1]. CELF reduces repeated marginal evaluations using
objective structure [6]; StaticGreedy reuses fixed realizations [3]; IMM develops
a sampling-based solver with approximation guarantees [2]. These approaches
motivate careful treatment of the sampled objective. We use exhaustive per-round
enumeration to isolate the effect of seed eligibility under a common finite-world
objective.
Ranking and search strategies offer complementary routes to efficient selection.
Neighbour-scale centrality [7] and discrete metaheuristic optimization [8], both stud-
ied inComputing, change how influential seeds are identified. Learned inductive es-
timators such as MONSTOR+ predict spread on unseen graphs [9]. Our comparisons
retain one simulator and greedy rule while changing the candidate pool, allowing
differences to be attributed to eligibility and evaluation resolution.
Benchmarking has established the importance of comparable models and imple-
mentation scope [5]. Temporal-network formulations [10] and empirical influence
models [11] also distinguish observed relations from diffusion assumptions. Our ev-
idence concerns simulated spread on observed topologies. The evaluation jointly
tracks reference budget, ranking precision, coverage, seed agreement, independent
spread and cost. Online Resource 1 provides a source-scoped comparison of these
dimensions.
3 Methods
3.1 Paired selection at controlled resolutions
LetG= (V,E)be a directed propagation graph andSa seed set of sizek. Under
the independent-cascade model, each newly activated vertex gets one opportunity to
activate each inactive outgoing neighbour with probabilityp. Propagation stops after
hrounds. For a random worldω, letf h(S;ω)denote total activated vertices, including
seeds. Greedy selection uses the sampled objective
bσW (S) = 1W W∑w=1 fh(S;ω w).(1)
At each round, greedy selection enumerates every unselected eligible vertex and
chooses the largest integer totalW bσW . Ties resolve by lexicographic original node
ID. Full selection usesC=V; restricted selection usesC⊆V. All vertices remain in
the propagation graph. Both arms shareG, the world prefix,kand the tie rule.
We vary reference resolution throughWand ranking resolution through the nu-
merical toleranceτ. Degree ranks distinct outgoing neighbours. PageRank uses the
unweighted directed topology and NetworkX 3.5 defaults: damping .85, uniform per-
sonalization and dangling-node treatment, tolerance 10 −6 and at most 100 iterations
[12]. Its stopping criterion is successive-vectorL 1 change belownτ, wheren=|V|.
Scores are sorted decreasingly with lexical ties. A top-qpool containsq=min(n,4k)

where such counts exist. Three random pools are independently generated within

3.2 World coupling and independent evaluation
Primary selection worlds share draws indexed by world, propagation step, source and
target. This coupling preserves the independent-cascade marginal law for each fixed
seed set, but the realized finite-world objective can be nonmonotone. For example,
initially activating an intermediate vertex can move its outgoing attempt to an earlier
step with a different sampled outcome. Online Resource 1 gives the explicit coun-
terexample. A separate fixed-edge control draws once per edge and world; its finite
objective is coverage by vertices reachable withinhedges and is monotone and sub-
modular. Both implementations use exhaustive enumeration.
Selection-world identifiers form nested prefixes asWincreases. Evaluation uses a
disjoint family ofM=10,000 worlds per path, shared by all seed sets and held fixed
across budgets. Keys also separate graphs, paths and candidate-pool randomization.
Coupling schemes are compared within their own full references.
For restricted seedsS C and full-reference seedsS F, define the paired evaluation
difference on worldiasd i =f h(SC;η i)−f h(SF;η i). The utility gap in Fig. 1 is
b∆= 1M M∑i=1 di,SE MC =s(d i)/√M.(2)
The interval b∆±1.96SE MC describes conditional Monte Carlo uncertainty for fixed
selected sets. Across-path standard deviation describes variation across computa-
tional paths, including finite evaluation noise. The three random-pool evaluation vec-
tors are averaged within each path before either calculation.
We report ordered-trajectory agreement, final-set agreement, Jaccard similarity
and the first divergent round. Incremental spread subtractskfrom total spread; per-
centage incremental loss is−100 b∆/( bσfull −k). Negative loss favors the restricted set.
All observed denominators are positive. The frozen primary tests compare degree-4k,
PageRank-4k and the within-path random mean with full selection atW=1280, us-
ing two-sided exact sign tests and Holm correction across three comparisons within
each graph. Zero differences are excluded and reported. Coverage, coupling and tran-
sition analyses are descriptive; worlds and paths are not independent network sam-
ples.

4.3 Randomization, execution and reproducibility
Randomization is specified by the configuration namespace, graph ID, path ID, world
family and zero-based world index. The frozen implementation hashes this tuple with
SHA-256 to obtain each 63-bit world identifier. Thus increasingWappends worlds
without resampling the prefix. The selection and evaluation families have distinct
keys; their identifiers are explicitly checked for overlap. A separate hash key con-
taining graph, path and repetition index generates each random candidate pool. The
pool remains fixed across budgets and coupling controls. Exact key strings and the
hash-to-draw mapping are preserved in the source snapshot and described in Online
Resource 1.
Experiments use one serial worker on Windows with Python 3.13.0, NumPy
2.3.3, NetworkX 3.5, SciPy 1.16.1 and Numba 0.68.0. BLAS/OpenMP threads are
constrained to one. JIT warm-up precedes timed conditions; randomized arm order
reduces systematic order effects. The recorded host has 20 physical CPU cores and
approximately 32 GiB RAM, although the implementation does not use all cores.
Timing and process peak-memory records accompany each condition. Exact wall
times are machine-dependent.
The reproduction entry point verifies frozen source and data hashes, rebuilds the
processed graphs and checks the result matrix before analysis. It supports a represen-
tative replay or the complete external matrix, writes to a new directory and compares
selected sequences, integer round scores and all evaluation counts with the archived
arrays. A separate stage regenerates default and precision-control statistics and plots
in an isolated copy. Numerical tables are checked against archived values, and raster
plots against decoded pixels; PDF timestamps and fresh execution times are not ex-
pected to match byte for byte. The supplied guide distinguishes these checks from
rerunning the historical experiments and the full post-hoc control.

5.2 Ranking precision resolves an apparent budget reversal
Figure 2 separates the effects of selection budget and ranking precision. On ca-GrQc,
default PageRank-4k changes from a 5.61% gain atW=80 to losses of 2.53% and
5.07% at 320 and 1280. Tightening tolerance exchanges two of 20 candidates and
yields gains of 11.25%, 2.96% and 0.54%. The original gain-to-loss reversal thus
depends on the numerical ranking.
Budget still matters under the stable strict pool. Its selected sets change on 3/5
paths from 80 to 320 and 2/5 from 320 to 1280. AtW=1280, the mean paired
difference is only+0.02608 nodes, with path values from−0.0408 to+0.1789 and
path standard deviation 0.09155. The mean gain is small relative to this variation. A
change in sign alone would obscure that scale.
CollegeMsg’s default and strict pools, seeds and evaluations are identical at every
budget, retaining losses of 9.33%, 8.52% and 9.01%. On wiki-V ote, one candidate
changes, but strict losses remain 74.05%, 74.05% and 73.96%. Precision sensitivity
is therefore network-specific: it changes the ca-GrQc interpretation while preserving
the two directed-network losses.

6 Discussion
The results show why reference budget and numerical ranking precision should be
controlled separately. Increasing the selection budget changes the sampled greedy
computation; tightening ranking tolerance can change which vertices enter that com-
putation. On ca-GrQc, a two-candidate exchange removes the default quality reversal
while leaving budget-dependent seed changes. On the directed graphs, tighter rank-
ing preserves the losses. Evaluating only one resolution would merge these different
explanations.
Three reporting choices follow. First, report the candidate fraction and actual
ranking tolerance alongside the reference budget. Second, distinguish ordered agree-
ment, final-set agreement and independently evaluated utility, including both con-
ditional uncertainty and path variation. Third, state which stages a cost comparison
includes. Together these quantities show whether a restriction preserves a reference
computation, retains its spread, or merely reduces search time.
The evidence covers three selected external networks underk=5,p=.01,h=3
and selection budgets up to 1280. The five paths are computational replicates; the
graphs do not form a probability sample of social systems. Observed messages, coau-
thorships and votes provide topology, not calibrated causal diffusion. Other seed
budgets, propagation laws, edge probabilities and orientations may change the re-
sults. Reference agreement does not establish expected-optimality or convergence,
and the paired numerical control remains post-hoc. Finally, cost savings are relative

7 Conclusion
Candidate-restriction quality depends on both the finite greedy reference and the pre-
cision of the truncated ranking. Our paired evaluation identifies a solver-sensitive
budget reversal, tests its external scope, and separates seed-set changes from utility
and cost. Degree-based pools reproduce all highest-budget external references at sub-
stantially lower selection cost, while PageRank’s precision and topology sensitivity
qualify its earlier small-graph agreement. Reporting these quantities together makes
quality–cost conclusions interpretable and reproducible.
