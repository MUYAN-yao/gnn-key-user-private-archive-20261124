Title: Reference Budgets and Ranking Precision in Candidate-Restricted Influence Maximization

Contribution and scope
This evaluation study examines two numerical choices that enter candidate-restricted greedy influence maximization at different stages: the sampled-world budget defining the full-candidate reference and the precision of the ranking defining seed eligibility. Its empirical contribution is to identify a precision dependence in an apparent budget-induced quality reversal, relate reference-set changes to independently evaluated utility, and delimit the transfer of candidate-restriction results across observed topologies.

Findings
On ca-GrQc, default PageRank-4k changes from a mean incremental-spread gain of 5.61% at W=80 to a loss of 5.07% at W=1280. A paired post-hoc tolerance control removes this reversal while leaving budget-dependent seed changes. CollegeMsg and wiki-Vote retain losses with a tighter PageRank convergence tolerance. Between W=320 and W=1280, all five ca-GrQc full-reference sets change, with a mean independent spread gain of 0.12720 nodes. At W=1280, 20-node degree-based pools reproduce all 15 external full-reference trajectories; measured selection savings are 98.40–99.67% relative to exhaustive full-candidate selection in the same implementation.

Evaluation
The frozen external matrix comprises three observed topologies, five computational paths, selection budgets 80/320/1280, shared selection-world prefixes and 10,000 disjoint evaluation worlds. The setting fixes k=5, p=.01 and horizon 3. Fixed-edge controls, candidate coverage and explicit timing scopes qualify the main comparisons. The 45 post-hoc precision conditions reuse the original paths and do not enlarge the independent sample. Separate historical contact-graph evidence covers local probability/horizon changes, fixed-edge sampling and larger seed pools. In the historical fixed-edge comparison, 29/40 reference sets change between W=320 and 1280, with a mean independent spread gain of 0.061845 nodes. These controls complement the external findings; they do not establish broader external parameter coverage or a joint strict-PageRank/fixed-edge result.

Interpretation and reproducibility
The study reports trajectory agreement, set agreement, conditional Monte Carlo uncertainty, path variation and computational cost separately. The direction diagnostic is observational; reference agreement does not establish optimality with respect to expected spread or equivalence. Frozen sources, input hashes, per-world outputs and analysis scripts support inspection. The 26 October reproduction check regenerated the external analyses and replayed 12 representative conditions (64 arms). The historical-evidence revision recomputed descriptive summaries from all 520 saved condition pairs without new experiments or simulator replay. The archived records distinguish stored-result verification, analysis regeneration and representative reruns; they do not establish a complete independent reproduction. Raw third-party datasets are obtained from their cited providers and are not redistributed.
