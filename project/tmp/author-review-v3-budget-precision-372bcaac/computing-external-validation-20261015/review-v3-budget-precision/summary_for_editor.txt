Title: Evaluating Candidate-Restricted Influence Maximization: Reference Budgets, Ranking Precision, and External Topologies

Contribution and distinction
This is an evaluation study of candidate eligibility under a finite-world greedy reference. It does not propose a new influence-maximization algorithm or claim a new discovery that sampling affects greedy decisions. Its contribution is to separate two evaluation resolutions that are often conflated: the number of sampled worlds defining a full-candidate reference, and the numerical tolerance used to truncate a ranking before greedy selection. It follows each resolution through candidate pools, seed sequences, independently evaluated reach, coverage and scoped costs.

Evaluation
An initial 42-condition archive and a separately frozen 520-condition extension remain distinct historical layers. An outcome-blind resource preflight preceded a locally frozen external matrix of 54 conditions and 468 arm executions on CollegeMsg, ca-GrQc and wiki-Vote, with 1,438–7,115 retained nodes. The external setting uses k=5, probability .01, horizon3, five paths, selection budgets80/320/1280 and 10,000 independent evaluation worlds per path. Full eligibility is compared with degree, standard PageRank, random pools and authentic historical-event ranking where available; fixed20-node and5% pools and preselected fixed-edge controls are retained.

At W1280, degree-4k reproduces 15/15 ordered references; default-tolerance PageRank-4k does not. Its mean incremental losses are 5.07% on ca-GrQc, 9.01% on CollegeMsg and 73.01% on wiki-Vote. After the W1280 precision control exposed a solver-sensitive ca-GrQc loss, a second separately frozen post-hoc control added W80/W320 strict PageRank results on the same paths: 30 conditions and 60 arms. Default ca-GrQc PageRank-4k changes from a 5.61% gain at W80 to a 5.07% loss at W1280, whereas a strict pool stable at tolerance 10^-14 remains a gain (-11.25%, -2.96%, -0.54%). CollegeMsg is identical under both tolerances; wiki-Vote retains 74.05%, 74.05% and 73.96% strict 4k losses. Thus budget still changes candidate sets and utility magnitude, but the default direction reversal is not precision-robust. Six of 15 full references change between W320 and 1280, with graph- and path-specific utility changes.

Validation and boundaries
The author-review package independently checks raw/graph/source/result hashes, integer argmax and ties, nested/disjoint random IDs, complete full-reference score replay, all evaluation arrays, and a separate scalar oracle on predetermined worlds. All primary comparisons and adverse outcomes are retained. The frozen per-graph sign tests have limited resolution with five paths and no Holm-significant comparisons; measured effects are not converted into equivalence claims. Timing scenarios distinguish measured selection from assembled setup/verification costs. External budgets stop at1280 and no convergence claim is made. Simulated outcomes do not establish real-world cascade performance, a universal safe pool fraction, or superiority over optimized IM systems.
