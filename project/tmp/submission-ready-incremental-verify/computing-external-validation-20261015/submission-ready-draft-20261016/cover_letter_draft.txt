Dear Editors of Computing,

Please consider our manuscript, “Evaluating Candidate-Restricted Influence Maximization: Reference Budgets, Ranking Precision, and External Topologies,” for publication in Computing.

The paper examines how candidate restrictions in greedy influence maximization should be evaluated when the full-eligibility reference is itself defined by finitely sampled diffusion worlds and a ranking is truncated numerically before selection. Rather than proposing another seed-selection algorithm, we separate reference budget and ranking tolerance as distinct evaluation resolutions, then follow their consequences for candidate pools, seed sequences, independently evaluated spread, coverage and scoped computational cost.

The frozen external study covers communication, collaboration and voting networks with 1,438--7,115 retained vertices. At W=1280, a 20-node outgoing-degree pool reproduces all 15 stored full-greedy trajectories, while default PageRank-4k has graph-specific incremental-spread losses of 5.07%, 9.01% and 73.01%. A separately frozen post-hoc tolerance control clarifies the interpretation: ca-GrQc’s default gain-to-loss pattern across budgets is not robust to PageRank tolerance, whereas CollegeMsg retains its loss and wiki-Vote retains a large strict-ranking loss. The manuscript presents this qualification prominently and does not treat the 45 post-hoc conditions as independent confirmation.

We believe Computing is a suitable venue because the work contributes transparent computational evaluation evidence for network algorithms. It combines frozen condition-level outputs, independent evaluation worlds, source and data hashes, replayable score checks, a scalar oracle on predetermined worlds, machine-readable analyses and supplementary tables. The accompanying novelty and evaluation summary explains the distinction from algorithm proposals, GNN methods and general-purpose benchmarking studies.
