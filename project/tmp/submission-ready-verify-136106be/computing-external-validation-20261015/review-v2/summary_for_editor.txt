Title: Candidate-Restricted Influence Maximization: Reference Resolution, Coverage, and External Validation

Contribution and distinction
This is an evaluation study of candidate eligibility under a finite-world greedy reference. It does not propose a new influence-maximization algorithm or claim a new discovery that sampling affects greedy decisions. It links exact ordered/set reproduction, independent total and incremental reach, actual candidate coverage, coupling semantics and attributed computational cost in a traceable protocol. The external evidence tests the transfer of earlier small-contact-network observations and retains findings that limit that transfer.

Evaluation
An initial 42-condition archive and a separately frozen 520-condition extension remain distinct historical layers. An outcome-blind resource preflight preceded a locally frozen external matrix of 54 conditions and 468 arm executions on CollegeMsg, ca-GrQc and wiki-Vote, with 1,438–7,115 retained nodes. The external setting uses k=5, probability .01, horizon3, five paths, selection budgets80/320/1280 and 10,000 independent evaluation worlds per path. Full eligibility is compared with degree, standard PageRank, random pools and authentic historical-event ranking where available; fixed20-node and5% pools and preselected fixed-edge controls are retained.

At W1280, degree-4k reproduces 15/15 ordered references; default-tolerance PageRank-4k does not. Its mean incremental losses are 5.07% on ca-GrQc, 9.01% on CollegeMsg and 73.01% on wiki-Vote. A separately frozen post-hoc numerical-precision control reruns both PageRank pool sizes on all graphs and original paths (15 auxiliary conditions, 30 arms). Tightening tolerance changes ca-GrQc's mean difference from −0.25082 to +0.02608 nodes: that original small loss is not precision-robust. CollegeMsg is unchanged; wiki-Vote still loses 73.96%, and its tightened 5% pool still loses 24.47%. Incoming importance and outgoing simulation differ in orientation; diagnostics support that qualification without establishing a causal explanation. Six of 15 final references change between 320 and 1280; their utility changes differ by graph and path.

Validation and boundaries
The author-review package independently checks raw/graph/source/result hashes, integer argmax and ties, nested/disjoint random IDs, complete full-reference score replay, all evaluation arrays, and a separate scalar oracle on predetermined worlds. All primary comparisons and adverse outcomes are retained. The frozen per-graph sign tests have limited resolution with five paths and no Holm-significant comparisons; measured effects are not converted into equivalence claims. Timing scenarios distinguish measured selection from assembled setup/verification costs. External budgets stop at1280 and no convergence claim is made. Simulated outcomes do not establish real-world cascade performance, a universal safe pool fraction, or superiority over optimized IM systems.
