Novelty and scope statement

This manuscript studies how to evaluate candidate-restricted greedy influence maximization when the comparator is a finite-world full-eligibility reference. Its central contribution is empirical and methodological: it distinguishes reference budget from numerical ranking precision, and shows how either choice can affect candidate membership, selected sequences, set agreement and independently evaluated spread.

The work differs from a conventional influence-maximization algorithm paper because it does not introduce a new optimizer, approximation guarantee, or faster implementation claim. It uses exhaustive finite-world greedy selection as a traceable comparator and reports the computational scope explicitly. It differs from a GNN or learned-influence paper because no model is trained, tuned or claimed to generalize across networks. It differs from a generic benchmarking paper because the primary question is the interpretation of a candidate restriction under changed reference resolution and ranking precision, with coupled worlds and disjoint evaluation worlds retained for that purpose.

The evidence is deliberately scoped. The 54-condition/468-arm external matrix is frozen; the 45-condition/90-arm PageRank precision study is post-hoc and reuses the original paths. The manuscript reports this status, preserves adverse results and does not infer a universal candidate fraction, expected-optimality, convergence, real-world cascade accuracy or population-wide network effect. Its practical recommendation is narrower: report the named reference budget, ranking tolerance, candidate coverage, trajectory/set agreement, independently evaluated utility and cost scenario together when candidate restrictions are assessed.
